Identifying Similar Text Sequences Using Cosine Similarity

Cosine similarity is one of the most popular and effective metrics used in Natural Language Processing (NLP) to measure how similar two sequences of text are, irrespective of their size.

This guide covers the intuition behind cosine similarity, how to prepare your text, and how to implement it in Python.

1. What is Cosine Similarity?

Mathematically, cosine similarity measures the cosine of the angle between two non-zero vectors projected in a multi-dimensional space.

In the context of text analysis, a document or sentence is converted into an array of numbers (a vector). Cosine similarity then checks the angle between these vectors.

An angle of $0^\circ$ (cosine similarity of $1$) means the texts are perfectly similar.

An angle of $90^\circ$ (cosine similarity of $0$) means they share no similarities.

The formula is defined as the dot product of the vectors divided by the product of their magnitudes:

$$\text{Cosine Similarity}(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}) = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}}{\|\mathbf{A}\| \|\mathbf{B}\|}$$

Where:

$\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}$ is the dot product of vectors $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.

$\|\mathbf{A}\|$ and $\|\mathbf{B}\|$ are the Euclidean lengths (magnitudes) of the vectors.

2. The Process

To compare text using cosine similarity, you cannot just pass raw strings into the equation. You need to follow a three-step pipeline:

Step A: Text Preprocessing

Clean the text sequences to ensure noise doesn't affect the similarity score. Standard steps include:

Lowercasing all text.

Removing punctuation and special characters.

Removing stop words (e.g., "the", "is", "in").

Lemmatization or stemming (reducing words to their root form, e.g., "running" $\rightarrow$ "run").

Step B: Vectorization

Convert the text into numerical vectors. Common methods include:

Bag of Words (CountVectorizer): Counts the frequency of each word.

TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency): Weighs word frequencies by how rare they are across all documents. This is generally preferred over standard Bag of Words.

Word Embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe, BERT): Uses neural networks to represent words as dense vectors, capturing deep semantic meaning rather than just word overlap.

Step C: Calculation

Apply the cosine similarity mathematical formula to the generated vectors.

3. Python Implementation (using TF-IDF)

Here is a practical example using Python's popular scikit-learn library. We will use TF-IDF for vectorization.

from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfVectorizer
from sklearn.metrics.pairwise import cosine_similarity
import pandas as pd

# 1. Define our text sequences (corpus)
documents = [
    "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.",
    "A fast brown fox leaps over a sleepy dog.",
    "Artificial intelligence is transforming the world of technology.",
    "The dog is lazy and sleepy."
]

# 2. Initialize the TF-IDF Vectorizer
# We can tell it to automatically remove english stop words
vectorizer = TfidfVectorizer(stop_words='english')

# 3. Fit and transform the texts into vectors
tfidf_matrix = vectorizer.fit_transform(documents)

# 4. Calculate the cosine similarity matrix
similarity_matrix = cosine_similarity(tfidf_matrix, tfidf_matrix)

# 5. Display the results nicely using a Pandas DataFrame
df = pd.DataFrame(
    similarity_matrix, 
    index=[f"Doc {i+1}" for i in range(len(documents))],
    columns=[f"Doc {i+1}" for i in range(len(documents))]
)

print("Cosine Similarity Matrix:\n")
print(df.round(3)) # Rounding to 3 decimal places for readability


Understanding the Output

When you run the code above, Doc 1 and Doc 2 will have a relatively high similarity score because they share similar contexts and words. Doc 3 will have a similarity score of exactly $0.0$ with the others because it shares no non-stop-words with the animal-related sentences.

4. Pros and Cons

Advantages

Magnitude Independent: It measures the orientation, not the magnitude. A 10-page document and a 1-page summary can have a high cosine similarity if they use the same vocabulary.

Efficient: It is computationally fast, especially with sparse matrices generated by TF-IDF.

Limitations

Lexical Gap (with TF-IDF/BoW): If you only use frequency-based vectors, "car" and "automobile" are treated as completely different dimensions. To fix this, use modern semantic embeddings (like OpenAI embeddings or Sentence-BERT) as your vectorization step before applying cosine similarity.

Ignores Word Order: "The dog bit the man" and "The man bit the dog" will yield identical TF-IDF vectors and thus a cosine similarity of $1.0$, even though the meanings are completely different. (Again, contextual embeddings like BERT solve this).

Summary

Cosine similarity is a fundamental, robust tool for text matching. While frequency-based methods like TF-IDF are great for keyword overlap, combining cosine similarity with modern transformer-based embeddings provides state-of-the-art semantic search capabilities.
